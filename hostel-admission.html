Allegations of Unofficial Payments for Government Hostel Admissions Raise Public Concern

By Bullet News Investigations Team • July 2026 • Hyderabad

Bullet News has received multiple reports from parents, students, and members of the public alleging that certain individuals are demanding unofficial payments ranging from ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 while claiming they can secure admissions into government residential hostels.

According to information shared with our newsroom, these individuals allegedly approach parents claiming to have influence over the admission process. Families are reportedly told that once a child receives admission, they "will not have to worry for the next six to seven years" because education, food, and accommodation are provided by the hostel system.

At present, Bullet News has not independently verified these allegations, and no conclusion should be drawn regarding any institution or individual without proper investigation.

Why These Allegations Matter

Government residential hostels exist to provide quality education and equal opportunities to deserving students, especially those from economically weaker backgrounds. If unofficial payments are being demanded by anyone claiming influence over admissions, such practices could undermine the principles of fairness, transparency, and merit that these institutions are meant to uphold.

Reports From Different Institutions

Some of the information received by Bullet News refers to concerns involving certain government residential hostels, including reports mentioning TGMREIS institutions and other residential education systems. Bullet News is continuing to collect information and verify these claims before reaching any conclusions.

Call for Transparency

Bullet News respectfully urges the concerned education authorities to examine any credible complaints related to hostel admissions. A fair and transparent inquiry would help maintain public confidence while ensuring deserving students receive equal opportunities through the proper admission process.

  • Admissions should be based only on official procedures.
  • Parents should avoid making unofficial payments.
  • Verified complaints should be investigated fairly.
  • Honest institutions and officials should also be protected through transparent inquiries.
